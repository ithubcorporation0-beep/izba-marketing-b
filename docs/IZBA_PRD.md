## 1. Project Overview

### 1.1 Product vision

Build a professional, modern, responsive website for **IZBA Marketing agency** that presents its IT, software, digital marketing, AI, design, data, digital product, video production, and business support services. The website should build trust, explain offerings clearly, showcase verified work, and generate qualified enquiries.

### 1.2 Business goals

1. Explain the company and its service offerings in a clear, organized way.
2. Generate qualified leads through quote-request and consultation forms.
3. Help visitors discover relevant services without overwhelming them with a flat list of 90 items.
4. Showcase only real, authorized portfolio work, testimonials, and results.
5. Support search engine discovery, mobile usability, accessibility, and performance.
6. Optionally allow authorized staff to update services, projects, articles, FAQs, and leads through an admin dashboard.

### 1.3 Target audiences

- Small and medium-sized businesses seeking websites, marketing, software, or automation.
- Startups and organizations building a digital presence or internal tools.
- Local businesses seeking local SEO, social media marketing, lead generation, or creative assets.
- Creators and brands seeking AI-assisted video, images, design, or content production.
- Businesses needing data entry, office support, IT consulting, or technical maintenance.
- Buyers interested in digital products and templates.

### 1.4 Product principles

- Honest marketing: never invent clients, reviews, certifications, awards, results, or company facts.
- Mobile-first, responsive, accessible, and fast.
- Reusable components and maintainable code.
- Every visible button and form must work.
- Keep service content editable; mark unconfirmed offerings as drafts until the owner approves them.
- Implement one phase at a time and verify it before proceeding.

## 2. Scope

### 2.1 MVP — first launch

- Responsive public marketing website.
- Home, About, Services, Service Detail, Portfolio, Contact, Privacy Policy, and optional Terms pages.
- Ten main service categories and an organized directory of detailed services (see Section 6).
- Search or category filtering for the service directory if it improves usability.

- SEO metadata, sitemap, robots.txt, favicon, and social-sharing metadata.
- Loading, success, error, empty, and not-found states.
- Production deployment with domain/HTTPS and a documented handover.

### 2.2 Phase 2 — recommended

- Secure admin/CMS for service categories, service pages, projects, FAQs, testimonials, and articles.
- Lead inbox with statuses, assignment, internal notes, search, and CSV export.
- Blog/insights section.
- Email notifications and optional CRM integration.
- Digital products catalog/store only if the owner confirms actual products and checkout requirements.

### 2.3 Out of scope unless separately approved

- Online payment processing, subscriptions, or invoicing.
- Customer portal or client login.
- Automatic operation of ad campaigns.
- Guaranteed SEO rankings, leads, or sales.
- Native mobile apps.

## 8. Functional Requirements

### 8.1 Service directory

- Services are grouped into the 10 categories above.
- Each service has a unique slug, name, category, short description, detail content, status, and optional SEO fields.
- Only published services appear publicly.
- Service names and category relationships are consistent across navigation, cards, forms, and detail pages.
- Owner can designate featured services separately from the complete directory.

### 8.2 Enquiry / quote form

Recommended fields:

- Full name (required).
- Business email (required; format validation).
- Phone/WhatsApp (optional unless owner requires it).
- Company/brand (optional).
- Service/category of interest (required; populated from published services/categories).
- Budget range (optional, owner-approved choices).
- Project details/message (required, length-limited).
- Consent checkbox where required by the selected privacy/communication approach.

Behavior:

- Validate on both client and server.
- Display field-level, accessible errors.
- Prevent repeated clicks/duplicate submissions while processing.
- Show success only after the server confirms acceptance.
- Show a useful retry message when submission fails.
- Apply rate limiting and spam protection (e.g. honeypot; CAPTCHA only if needed).
- Deliver to a confirmed email/CRM destination and/or store securely.
- Never expose API keys or private credentials in browser code.

### 8.3 Calls to action

- Request a Quote opens the quote form.
- Explore Services navigates to the service directory.
- Book a Consultation uses an owner-approved booking tool or the enquiry form.
- Phone, email, and WhatsApp links use confirmed values only.

### 8.4 Admin/CMS — Phase 2 or owner-approved MVP

- Secure login/logout; no public registration unless required.
- Create, edit, publish, unpublish, archive, and delete categories/services.
- Manage projects, articles, FAQs, testimonials, and selected page content.
- Manage leads and status changes; export only for authorized roles.
- Search, filtering, pagination, and confirmation for destructive actions.
- Record audit history for important content and permission changes.
- Enforce permissions on the server and database, not only in the UI.

## 11. Recommended Technical Architecture

- Frontend/full-stack: Next.js with TypeScript.
- Styling: Tailwind CSS and reusable accessible components.
- Database/authentication for CMS: Supabase (PostgreSQL and Supabase Auth), if needed.
- Hosting: Vercel or compatible provider.
- Email: owner-approved transactional email provider.
- Version control: Git and GitHub.
- Validation: server-side schema validation at all external input boundaries.
- Use the existing repository stack if already established; inspect before changing.

Architecture rules:

- TypeScript strict mode where practical.
- Server components by default where appropriate; client components only for required interactivity.
- Separate UI, content, validation, data access, and business logic.
- Keep secrets in environment variables and never commit secret values.
- Use database migrations for schema changes.
- Avoid unnecessary dependencies and full rewrites.

## 12. Database Schema (when CMS or persistent leads are required)

Use PostgreSQL/Supabase or equivalent. Add timestamps, indexes, constraints, and migrations.

### `service_categories`

- `id` UUID primary key.
- `name` text, required.
- `slug` text, required and unique.
- `description` text.
- `sort_order` integer.
- `is_published` boolean default false.
- `created_at`, `updated_at` timestamptz.

### `services`

- `id` UUID primary key.
- `category_id` UUID foreign key to `service_categories`.
- `name` text, required.
- `slug` text, required and unique.
- `short_description` text.
- `description` text or structured content.
- `deliverables` JSONB or structured related table, optional.
- `is_featured` boolean default false.
- `status` constrained to `draft`, `published`, `archived`.
- `sort_order` integer.
- `seo_title`, `seo_description` text.
- `created_at`, `updated_at` timestamptz.

### `projects`

- `id` UUID primary key; `title` and unique `slug` required.
- `client_name` nullable; publish only with permission.
- `category` or related service/category ID.
- `summary`, `challenge`, `approach`, `outcome` text.
- `metrics` JSONB optional and verified before publication.
- `cover_image_url` text.
- `is_published` boolean default false; `published_at` timestamptz.
- `created_at`, `updated_at` timestamptz.

### `enquiries`

- `id` UUID primary key.
- `full_name`, `email`, `message` required text.
- `phone`, `company`, `budget_range`, `source_page` optional text.
- `service_id` nullable UUID foreign key to `services`.
- `status` constrained to `new`, `contacted`, `qualified`, `won`, `closed`.
- `assigned_to` optional UUID referencing authorized staff profile.
- `created_at`, `updated_at` timestamptz.

### `profiles`

- `id` UUID primary key matching auth user ID.
- `display_name` text.
- `role` constrained to approved roles such as `admin` and `editor`.
- `created_at`, `updated_at` timestamptz.

### Optional `testimonials`, `faqs`, `articles`, and `digital_products`

- Each record needs a stable UUID, content fields, publication status, ordering/timestamps, and appropriate SEO fields where relevant.
- Testimonials require publication approval and permission.
- Digital products need clear price/currency, product file/storage permissions, license terms, and order/payment tables only if checkout is approved.

### Database access rules

- Enable and test Row Level Security (RLS) on exposed tables.
- Public visitors may read only published categories/services/projects/articles/products intended for public display.
- Public visitors must not read enquiries, profiles, or unpublished records.
- Create enquiries through a validated server endpoint or narrowly scoped secure policy.
- Enforce admin/editor access at server and database levels.
- Never expose service-role secrets to the browser.

## 13. Security and Privacy

- HTTPS in production.
- Use environment variables and a placeholder `.env.example`; never commit real secrets.
- Validate input on the server; limit payload size.
- Rate-limit forms and login; apply spam protection.
- Use secure auth/session practices and server-side authorization.
- Use parameterized database queries/safe data APIs.
- Sanitize or safely render user-generated content.
- Use suitable security headers and protect against XSS, CSRF where applicable, and open redirects.
- Restrict uploads by type, size, and access if uploads are added.
- Keep dependencies updated and scan for vulnerabilities.
- Collect only necessary personal data; restrict access to enquiries and define retention/deletion rules.
- Do not send email addresses, phone numbers, or message contents to analytics.
- Document third-party processors and keep the privacy policy accurate.
- Document backups and recovery for database-backed features.

## 14. SEO, Performance, and Analytics

### SEO

- Unique page titles and meta descriptions.
- Sitemap, robots.txt, canonical URLs where appropriate.
- Open Graph/social metadata.
- Semantic HTML, descriptive internal links, clean slugs, and redirects when URLs change.
- Optimized images and descriptive alt text.
- Organization/LocalBusiness structured data only for verified eligible details.
- No misleading schema or ranking guarantees.

### Performance

- Use optimized responsive images and avoid unnecessary client-side JavaScript.
- Lazy-load below-the-fold images when appropriate.
- Prevent layout shifts and use sensible caching.
- Test Core Web Vitals and mobile experience.
- Suggested Lighthouse performance target: 85+ initially, improving where practical; scores vary by test environment.

### Analytics

- Track useful events such as CTA clicks and successful enquiry submissions.
- Never send form contents or personal information to analytics.
- Configure consent where required by tools and applicable law.

## 22. Owner Checklist

### Before development

- [ ] Confirm official logo and brand preferences.
- [ ] Confirm contact details and service area.
- [ ] Confirm which services are currently deliverable and which are additional/on-request.
- [ ] Approve featured services and website copy.
- [ ] Confirm domain, hosting, and enquiry destination.
- [ ] Decide whether CMS/admin and digital product checkout are required at launch.

### Before launch

- [ ] Replace all placeholders.
- [ ] Test enquiry delivery end-to-end.
- [ ] Review privacy policy and consent settings.
- [ ] Approve portfolio assets and client permissions.
- [ ] Test mobile and keyboard accessibility.
- [ ] Check SEO/indexing settings and sitemap.
- [ ] Verify account ownership and handover documentation.

**End of PRD**
